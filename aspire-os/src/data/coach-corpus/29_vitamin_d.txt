## The Principle

Vitamin D is not actually a vitamin. It is a steroid hormone the body produces when sunlight hits the skin, then activates through the liver and kidney into its functional form. Calling it a "vitamin" is a historical accident from when researchers thought it was strictly a dietary nutrient.

The functional form — 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 — acts as a hormone in essentially every tissue in the body. There are vitamin D receptors in bone, muscle, brain, immune cells, gut, prostate, breast tissue, and many others. Deficiency does not produce one symptom; it produces a slow degradation of dozens of downstream systems.

The Aspire OS position on vitamin D is direct:

1. **Most adults in the developed world are deficient or insufficient** — particularly above ~37°N latitude, particularly in winter, particularly people with darker skin.
2. **The "normal" lab range is not the optimal range.** A 25-hydroxyvitamin D level of 25 ng/mL will be flagged as "normal" by most labs. The Aspire OS optimal is 40–60 ng/mL.
3. **Supplementation is necessary** in northern latitudes from roughly October through March — sun cannot synthesize D in those months at those latitudes regardless of how much time is spent outside.
4. **Test blood, don't guess.** A 25-hydroxyvitamin D test is one of the highest-value annual labs available — cheap, informative, actionable.

This is one of the very few areas where the protocol routes users to supplementation rather than lifestyle change first — because the lifestyle change (more sun) cannot fix the problem during the months it matters most.

## The Biology

### How the Body Makes Vitamin D

The synthesis pathway:

1. **UVB radiation** (290–315 nm wavelength) hits skin
2. **7-dehydrocholesterol** in the skin converts to **previtamin D3**
3. Body heat isomerizes previtamin D3 to **vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)**
4. D3 travels to the liver, where it is converted to **25-hydroxyvitamin D (25-OH-D)** — the storage form measured in bloodwork
5. The kidney converts 25-OH-D to **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol)** — the active hormone — as needed

Each step can become a bottleneck. People with kidney or liver disease may have adequate 25-OH-D but inadequate active form. Most adults, however, are bottlenecked at step 1–3: insufficient skin exposure to UVB.

### The Geographic Problem

UVB radiation reaches the skin only when the sun is above ~50° in the sky. Below that angle, UVB is filtered out by atmospheric thickness. This means:

- **In Toronto/Waterloo (43°N):** No meaningful vitamin D synthesis from approximately mid-October through mid-March. Even at noon. Even on a sunny day. The angle is simply too low.
- **In Vancouver (49°N):** Even longer deficient window — early September through April.
- **In Miami (25°N):** Year-round synthesis possible.
- **In Reykjavik (64°N):** Roughly 4 months of viable synthesis per year.

This is non-negotiable physics. No amount of outdoor time in January at 43°N produces vitamin D. Bundled-up outdoor exposure produces zero synthesis regardless of latitude — skin must be exposed to direct UVB.

Even in summer at viable latitudes, additional factors block synthesis:
- **Sunscreen** (SPF 30 blocks ~95% of UVB)
- **Window glass** (filters out UVB entirely)
- **Darker skin tones** (require longer exposure for equivalent synthesis)
- **Older age** (skin produces ~25% as much vitamin D at 70 as at 20)
- **Excess body fat** (sequesters vitamin D, reducing serum levels)

### The Bloodwork: 25-Hydroxyvitamin D

The single most informative test is **serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D**, often labeled "25-OH-D" or "Vitamin D, 25-hydroxy."

Interpretation ranges (where the brand takes a position):

| Range (ng/mL) | Standard interpretation | Aspire OS interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| <20 | Deficient | Deficient — clinical risk |
| 20–30 | Insufficient | Suboptimal — supplement aggressively |
| 30–40 | Normal | Still suboptimal — supplement |
| **40–60** | High-normal | **Optimal target range** |
| 60–80 | High | Acceptable; monitor |
| 80–100 | Very high | Reduce dose |
| >100 | Toxicity risk | Stop supplementation; investigate |

The 40–60 ng/mL target is what most longevity-focused researchers (Holick, Attia, others) recommend based on outcome data — not the 30 ng/mL "normal" floor most labs use, which is based on bone health alone and ignores immune, mood, and cardiovascular outcomes.

Test annually at minimum. Test more frequently after starting or changing a supplementation protocol.

### Supplementation: D3, K2, and Dosing

**D3 (cholecalciferol) is the form to use.** D2 (ergocalciferol) is plant-derived, less bioavailable, and produces lower serum levels at equivalent doses. D3 is the form the human body synthesizes naturally.

**Daily dosing for adults at ~43°N:**
- **Maintenance (summer, with sun exposure):** 1,000–2,000 IU/day or skip on consistently sunny days
- **Maintenance (winter, no sun synthesis):** 2,000–5,000 IU/day
- **Correction (deficient, starting low):** 5,000 IU/day for 8–12 weeks, then retest
- **Cap:** 10,000 IU/day for adults without medical supervision

Take with fat (a meal containing fat) for absorption — vitamin D is fat-soluble.

**Vitamin K2 co-supplementation.** Vitamin D increases calcium absorption from the gut. Vitamin K2 directs that calcium to bone and away from soft tissue (including arterial walls). Without K2, high-dose D supplementation may marginally increase arterial calcification risk in some populations. The conservative approach:

- For supplementation up to ~2,000 IU/day: K2 co-supplementation is optional
- For 2,000–5,000 IU/day: K2 (100–200 mcg MK-7 form) is recommended
- For 5,000+ IU/day: K2 is non-negotiable

K2 is found in fermented foods (natto especially) but in amounts difficult to reach through diet alone. MK-7 form has the longest half-life and is the standard supplement choice.

### What Vitamin D Affects Beyond Bones

The "vitamin D is for bones" framing is technically true but misses most of what the hormone does. Vitamin D receptors exist throughout the body. Documented effects of adequate vitamin D levels include:

- **Immune function** — appropriate inflammatory response, reduced respiratory infection severity
- **Mood regulation** — strong correlations with depression risk, particularly seasonal affective disorder
- **Muscle function** — adequate D is required for proper muscle contraction and reduced fall risk in older adults
- **Cardiovascular health** — associated with reduced cardiovascular event risk in observational studies
- **Glucose regulation** — vitamin D receptors in pancreatic beta cells; deficiency associated with insulin resistance
- **Cognitive function** — emerging research linking adequate D to reduced dementia risk
- **Hormone production** — some testosterone and thyroid pathways involve vitamin D

The Coach treats vitamin D as a system-wide hormone, not as a bone-specific nutrient.

## The Application

When responding to users, the Coach uses vitamin D knowledge in three ways:

**1. Reframe "normal" lab results when they're suboptimal.**

*"Your bloodwork came back at 28 ng/mL and the lab marked it as 'normal'? The lab range is set for bone health alone. The optimal range for immune, mood, and cardiovascular outcomes is 40–60 ng/mL. You're not deficient — you're suboptimal. That's not a clinical problem, but it's leaving function on the table. Supplement 3,000–5,000 IU daily for 8 weeks and retest."*

**2. Anchor northern-latitude users to the supplementation reality.**

*"You're in Toronto and trying to get vitamin D from winter sun? The physics doesn't allow it. UVB doesn't reach the skin when the sun is below 50° — that's October through March at your latitude. No amount of outdoor time in January synthesizes vitamin D. Supplementation isn't optional for half the year — it's the only mechanism available."*

**3. Pair D with K2 for high-dose supplementation.**

*"If you're going above 2,000 IU of D3 daily, pair it with 100–200 mcg of K2 (MK-7 form). D pulls calcium from the gut. K2 directs where it goes — into bone, not arteries. The two work as a system. Taking high-dose D alone for years without K2 is the worst-case version of the supplementation."*
